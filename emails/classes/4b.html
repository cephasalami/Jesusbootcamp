Jesus Boot Camp Class 4.b

The Purpose of the New Testament

{{#if first_name}}Hi {{first_name}},{{else}}Hi there,{{/if}}

Too many believers say they believe in grace, yet they continue living as though God’s acceptance must be earned.

They measure themselves by rules, punish themselves for every failure, carry old shame, and keep trying to become righteous through religious effort. They believe Jesus saved them, but then spend the rest of their lives attempting to finish the work He already completed.

But the New Testament announces something radically different.

Jesus did not come to give humanity another religious system. He came to end our separation from the Father.

The Law revealed the problem, but it could not remove the sin nature. It exposed humanity’s guilt, but it could not recreate the human heart. No amount of discipline, good behavior, religious devotion, or self-improvement could repair what had been corrupted through the fall.

That is why God did what we could never do.

He provided the Lamb.

At the cross, Jesus absorbed the punishment, erased the debt, and declared, “It is finished.” His sacrifice was not a down payment. It was the complete and final settlement for sin.

You are not called to work your way toward God.

Through Christ, God has already brought you home.

This class will reveal the true purpose of the New Testament. You will discover why Christ is the end of the Law for righteousness, why salvation is received entirely by grace through faith, and how the cross moves you from guilt to forgiveness, from slavery to sonship, and from spiritual orphanhood into an intimate relationship with God as your Father.

You will also confront one of the enemy’s most destructive strategies: persuading believers to keep paying for failures Jesus already carried.

Stop dragging around the backpack of guilt.

Stop trying to earn the love that was demonstrated while you were still a sinner. Stop standing in the courtroom of self-condemnation when the Judge has already declared the debt paid in full.

The New Testament is not the story of humanity climbing toward God.

It is the story of God coming to rescue humanity and restore His children to Himself.
